How Property, FD and SIP Compare

Property, Fixed Deposits and SIPs have different risk, liquidity, taxation and return characteristics.

A property investment can potentially benefit from capital appreciation and rental income, but may also involve transaction costs, maintenance, taxes and lower liquidity.

An FD generally provides a predetermined interest rate for its chosen tenure, while a SIP invests periodically in market-linked investments whose returns are not guaranteed.

This calculator uses hypothetical return assumptions. It does not predict or guarantee actual investment returns and does not include taxes, brokerage, stamp duty, maintenance, rental income or all other investment costs unless specifically entered.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is property better than an FD?

There is no universal answer. The outcome depends on property appreciation, transaction costs, rental income, FD interest rates, taxes, holding period and other factors.

Is SIP better than property?

A SIP and property are very different investments. SIP returns are market-linked and not guaranteed, while property involves ownership of a physical asset and can generate rental income and capital appreciation.

Are SIP returns guaranteed?

No. SIPs are a method of investing periodically and the underlying market-linked investments can rise or fall.

Does this calculator include property rental income?

No. This basic comparison focuses on the assumed capital value of the property. Rental income and property expenses should be considered separately for a more complete property investment analysis.

Does the calculator include taxes?

No. Tax treatment varies between investments and individual circumstances, so the results are shown before applicable taxes.
