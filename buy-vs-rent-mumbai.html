How Buy vs Rent Works

Buying a property creates an asset, but it also involves a down payment, home loan interest and transaction costs.

Renting usually requires less upfront capital, but rent can increase over time and you do not build property ownership through rent payments.

This calculator compares the basic financial impact of the two choices using the assumptions you provide.

This is an estimate, not financial advice. Actual results can differ because of property prices, rent changes, interest rates, taxes, maintenance, investment returns and other costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is buying a property always better than renting?

No. The better option depends on property prices, rent, loan costs, expected appreciation, how long you plan to stay and what you could do with the money that would otherwise be used as a down payment.

Does this calculator include home loan EMI?

Yes. The calculator estimates EMI using the loan amount, interest rate and loan tenure you enter.

Does the calculator account for rent increases?

Yes. It estimates cumulative rent using the annual rent increase percentage you provide.

Does the calculator include property appreciation?

Yes. You can enter an expected annual appreciation rate to estimate the property's future value.

Is this a complete investment comparison?

No. This is a simplified comparison tool. It does not automatically account for every possible factor such as maintenance, taxes, insurance, opportunity cost or alternative investment returns.
